Basis. Adult cats sleep roughly 12–18 h per day, and more than a third exceed 18 h. Individual sleep bouts average about 78 minutes, commonly 50–113. Roughly a quarter of feline sleep is paradoxical (REM). Cats are crepuscular rather than nocturnal, with two activity peaks — one shortly before sunrise, one around sunset — and feral cats may spend upwards of 12 of every 24 hours locating and catching food, taken in many small meals across the whole day and night. Sunrise, sunset and solar noon are computed from the sunrise equation for the coordinates entered. Everything downstream of those figures — the bout placement, the inversion, the prescription — is invention with a straight face.

Not medical advice. Nobody has shown that eating on a cat's schedule does anything for a human. The one defensible claim here is that a fixed sleep time, a fixed wake time, and no food immediately before bed are reasonable habits, and that arriving at them via an imaginary cat does not make them less reasonable.

Colour: cat arousal is a single-hue ordinal ramp, five steps, monotone in lightness; the operator schedule uses three categorical hues. Both sets were checked against the light and dark surfaces for lightness banding, chroma, colour-vision separation and contrast. Every encoded value is also reachable in the tables above.
