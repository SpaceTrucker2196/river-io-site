01 · The problem

Filling clinical shifts is fragmented, opaque, and risky.

01
Fragmentation. A scheduler juggles a dozen agency portals, emails, and spreadsheets to cover one open shift.
02
Opaque markups. MSP/VMS intermediaries insert a house agency and skim; the buyer can't see the real bill-vs-pay spread.
03
Compliance exposure. Nothing structurally stops an under-credentialed or excluded worker from being placed — a patient-safety and liability landmine.
04
Coverage gaps. Shifts starting tonight go unfilled while bids sit unseen; the fallback is overtime and burnout.
05
Credentials don't travel. A nurse re-proves the same licenses to every agency; the wallet is trapped.
02 · Why it costs real money

The buyer pays more, moves slower, and carries risk they can't measure.

Unfilled shift

Patient risk, plus premium overtime or a last-minute premium-rate agency call.

Non-compliant placement

Regulatory exposure and reputational risk — the kind that ends contracts.

Hidden markup

Every dollar of intermediary skim is margin the facility and the clinician never see.

03 · The solution

Crane is the neutral marketplace — and the infrastructure under it.

Providers post shifts once. The shift is broadcast to the whole eligible agency panel atomically — one transaction, one timestamp, no agency favored. Agencies bid their workers; the credential gate hard-blocks anyone missing a required credential. The buyer awards the best compliant bid, sees the exact spread, and the money flows through GAAP-clean invoicing and payroll.

No house agency. No hidden markup. No un-credentialed placement.

05 · The moats

Five invariants — enforced in code, each with a test that is its oracle.

Competitors promise neutrality and compliance. Crane makes them structural.

01
Vendor-neutral broadcast. All agencies get the shift in one transaction with one identical timestamp — provable by query, not asserted.
02
Hard credential gate. A worker missing any required credential cannot be submitted or awarded. Never bypassed, never advisory.
03
Exact, auditable money. Every fee, invoice, and payout is integer cents traced to the graph edges — never estimated or silently rounded.
04
PHI stays in the vault. Documents, license numbers, even worker names are encrypted per-tenant; the graph holds only opaque references.
05
Tamper-evident audit. A hash-chained trail plus least-privilege, per-area access. SOC 2-style controls, built in.

10 · Security & compliance

Compliance by construction, not by policy PDF.

HIPAA

PHI never touches the marketplace graph — it's encrypted in a vault, decryptable only along a valid authorization edge.

SOC 2-style

Hash-chained audit log, role-based least-privilege access, per-endpoint authorization, credential provenance.

Continuous

The gate honors exclusion-list monitoring and re-checks at award — a credential that lapsed after submission is caught.
