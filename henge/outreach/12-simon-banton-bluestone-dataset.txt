The short answer is no. Henge doesn't hold that data.

I checked the code before replying. The bluestones in the app are generated
from a ring radius and a count, so every stone in the circle is 0.95 m by
0.62 m and every stone in the horseshoe is 0.9 by 0.6. Fifty-nine stones,
two cross-sections between them. The sarsen circle uses one width for all
thirty. Petrie numbers are assigned by position in a loop, so my stone 31
isn't reliably your stone 31. It's a reading of a plan rather than a survey,
and I'd rather say so than have you find it.

Since I couldn't answer from the app, I spent a couple of days on what is
actually out there. Some of this you'll know already.

The laser scan report's Appendix 1 has surface area and volume alongside the
weights you use (printed pages 59 and 60, report at
https://historicengland.org.uk/research/results/reports/32-2012). I tried to
get a width and thickness out of those by treating the surface area as a
prism's surface. It doesn't work. The quadratic you end up solving has a
discriminant of (w - t)^2, so it recovers the difference between the two
dimensions and reconstructs them from it, and for a near-square section any
error in the surface area takes it negative. On eight test stones, four
failed outright, three of them bluestones, and one returned a 26 cm thickness.
The surface-area column seems to be scanned visible faces rather than a
closed solid, so there's no perimeter in it.

What does come out cleanly is cross-sectional area, from volume over your
Cleal heights, and from that the equal-area circle:

    stone            area (m2)   circle d (m)
    1  sarsen          1.376        1.32
    16 sarsen          2.295        1.71
    53 sarsen          1.459        1.36
    56 sarsen          1.467        1.37
    31 bluestone       0.362        0.68
    49 bluestone       0.195        0.50
    62 bluestone       0.202        0.51
    68 bluestone       0.285        0.60

Stone 16 lands within half a percent of the 2.1 by 1.1 m usually quoted.
It's a mean over the above-ground height, not the at-ground figure, the
volumes are 2011 and the heights are 1919 and Atkinson, and Table 2 only
covers 24 bluestones with no Altar Stone. So it's a check, not the answer.

Three other things I found.

Tim's footprint data went up this morning (stonehenge-block-3d on GitHub,
CC BY-SA). Per stone it has width, thickness, footprint along and across,
and an accuracy class. It's digitised from the Rees 1989 sheet rather than
measured, and the accuracy classes say so honestly, but it's the first
per-stone footprint set anyone has published. You'll have seen it.

Petrie's 1880 book has a per-stone table on pages 10 to 12: height and top
level in inches for everything standing in 1877, and Plate I draws each
stone's horizontal section at ground level. Public domain, full view on
Google Books. Pre-restoration, so it's an independent check rather than a
current plan, but as far as I can tell nobody has digitised Plate I.

And the only route I found to measured at-ground sections is a request to
the Historic England Archive. Trevor Cox at Salford got a 157-stone CAD
model derived from the scan in 2019 for the acoustic model, and his 2020
paper ran statistics on 81 bluestones from it. So the model exists and has
been handed out with a research case behind it.

Two offers, no conditions on either. I'll run the volume-over-height
